# Pudgy Penguins — Data Request, Round 3

**What this is:** our next ask, focused on four specific things we still need more of: **character design sheets, motion clips, expression clips, and interaction clips between Pax & Polly.** 

## Why we're asking for these four things

- **Character design sheets** : we asked for these in our very first request and still don't have them. Without them, we've had to approximate what Pax and Polly look like from other footage, which isn't as reliable as the real reference art.
- **Motion clips** : everything we have so far is cut from finished skits, and every skit mixes several actions together with dialogue and camera movement. We've never had a clean clip of just "Pax waving" or "Polly walking" on its own.
- **Expression clips** : same issue: expressions only ever show up mixed into a full skit, never on their own.
- **Interaction clips** : shots of Pax and Polly together are the hardest thing for the model to get right, and it's the one area more footage helps the most directly.

**One thing worth calling out:** in our testing, **Polly comes out less consistent than Pax** : she drifts off-model more easily. The fix is simply more reference material of Polly specifically, not something we can fix on our end. If you can only prioritize one character first for #1 and #2 below, make it Polly.

## 1. Character design sheets

For **both** Pax (blue) and Polly (pink):

| What | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Turnaround** | The character standing neutrally, shown from about 8 angles : front, angled front-left/right, side-left/right, angled back-left/right, and straight back |
| **Expression sheet** | The character's face/pose for each of: neutral, happy, sad, angry, surprised, scared, confused, laughing, crying, affectionate, exasperated |
| **Colors** | Exact color codes and accessory names alongside the art, so we're never guessing at a shade |
| **File format** | Your best-quality artwork : PNG with a transparent background if possible, one pose per file, reasonably high resolution |
| **How many** | Roughly 25–50 images per character (50–100 total) |

## 2. Motion clips

Short clips made specifically for this, not pulled from a skit:

- **One action per clip**, camera doesn't move, simple background, just a few seconds long.
- **One character at a time** : not both together. This is what actually lets the model learn each motion cleanly, which mixed skit footage can't do.
- Actions we're missing or don't have enough of: walking, running, turning, sitting, standing/idle, jumping, waving, turning the head, bouncing.
- Where it makes sense, have the clip **end on the same pose it started on** (for walking/idle/bouncing) : this helps us make clean, seamless loops.
- Roughly 4–5 clips per action, per character is a good target.

## 3. Expression clips

- Short clips showing **neutral → the expression → hold** (not just a single still image) : since we're working with a video model, a brief clip of the expression happening is more useful than a static picture.
- Cover: happy, sad, angry, surprised, scared, confused, laughing, crying, affectionate/love : this matches emotions already implied by skit titles you've sent us (like "Scared" or "Angry"), just never captured on their own.
- Same setup as the motion clips: one character at a time, simple background.

## 4. Interaction clips (Pax & Polly together)

- Clean two-character clips, simple backgrounds where possible, so the interaction itself is the focus.
- Interactions we don't have much of yet: hugging, holding hands/flippers, high-five, back-to-back, piggyback (we have a little : more is welcome), doing the same action in sync.
- One clip that clearly shows their **relative size** side by side : Polly is meant to be slightly smaller than Pax, and it helps to have a clear reference for that.
- A mix of both **standing apart with space between them** and **close together/touching** : we have more of the "apart" type already, so lean toward "close together" if you're choosing.

## 5. Camera variety : zoom, focus, and angle

Almost everything sent so far is shot the same way: static camera, medium-to-wide, straight-on, eye level. That sameness limits how varied the output can look, no matter how good the footage quality is. For the motion, expression, and interaction clips in sections 2–4 (not the character design sheets, which already cover all their own angles), please mix in some variety:

| What to vary | How |
|---|---|
| **Zoom / how close the camera is** | Some **close-up** shots (face and shoulders : especially useful for expressions), some **medium** (waist-up), some **wide** (full body with some background) |
| **Focus** | Mostly everything sharp and in focus, as usual : but a few shots where something in the foreground or background is intentionally soft/blurry are welcome too (we've seen your team do this already in some skits, and it reads fine as long as it's a deliberate choice) |
| **Camera angle** | Mostly straight-on/eye-level as usual, but a few slightly-above or slightly-below angles help too. Please avoid extreme tilted angles unless a scene specifically calls for it |
| **Which way the character faces** | A mix of facing the camera directly, at an angle, and in profile : natural variety as they move, not the controlled turnaround poses from section 1 |
| **Vertical vs. horizontal video** | Vertical (phone-shaped, 9:16) is still our main format, but a few horizontal (16:9) clips are useful too, especially for wider two-character shots |

Rough rule of thumb: across sections 2–4, try not to have more than about half the clips share the exact same zoom level or angle.
